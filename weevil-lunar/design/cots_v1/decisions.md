# COTS-1 design decisions

Reviewed 2026-10-16. This record separates selected architecture from capabilities awaiting tests.

1. **Rotary, independently controlled legs.** The original screw-foot concept couples motions and relies on uncertain soil engagement. Three rotary axes give an observable foot-placement problem; retain the screw foot as an independent test article until anchoring is measured.
2. **High-ratio actuators over hobby servos.** Early comparison found ordinary hobby/compact servos' continuous torque inadequate for the Earth-gravity leg. The AK80-64 was selected on its 48 Nm catalog rating and documented interfaces. Its rated torque is still not a stationary-hold thermal guarantee. An integrated industrial actuator is a possible replacement if bench tests fail, not a drop-in equivalence claim.
3. **Five contacts, no free preload.** Equal weight/5 is insufficient on a slope. Full wrench allocation exposed severe load imbalance in earlier geometries. Moving the side hips to ±260 mm, selecting 160 mm pads and defining a force cap made the sampled equilibrium feasible. No artificial actuator normal force is added to rover weight.
4. **Independent bearings and full overturning moment.** A short hub-offset shaft calculation misses the yaw joint's bending load. The final screen uses a 400 mm force arm, 20 mm solid shafts, specified ≥900 MPa yield and a 60 mm bearing span. It includes all leg proxy mass. Detailed couplings and frame joints remain release gates.
5. **Explicit ground-prototype mass.** The initial 30 kg concept is incompatible with this instrumented commercial hardware. Iterations at 45, 50 and 55 kg were rejected as a final mass baseline after accounting for instrumentation, bearings, solid shaft lengths and protection hardware. The selected 60 kg ceiling leaves 6.265 kg against current allocations. It is not a 60 kg lunar flight proposal.
6. **Keep source voltage and auxiliary limits compatible.** Use the 36 V battery and 36 V bench variant. The auxiliary converter's 33.6 V continuous minimum invalidated an initial 32 V cutoff; the final cutoff is 34 V under load. Oscilloscope and brownout tests remain necessary.
7. **Size braking independently of a software power limit.** All-joint torque × speed bound is 233.3 W. The tolerance-adjusted resistor screen exceeds that bound even at the cutoff. A software 150 W limit is additional; no software promise establishes resistor pulse suitability or clamp behavior after power loss.
8. **Preserve honest deliverable boundaries.** The repository contains an offline analytical model, generated packaging STEP, interface coupons and a connected kinematic URDF. It does not contain a complete manufacturing assembly, tested controller, proof of dust tolerance or a flight-ready design. Remaining gates are explicit in `acceptance.md`.
