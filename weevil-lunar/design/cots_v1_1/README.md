# COTS-1.1: chassis corrections and support-loss interlocks

**Ten modeled overlaps are corrected; 60 unfinished leg-interface overlaps remain. Fabrication and hardware motion remain blocked.** This revision addresses the actionable chassis errors in COTS-1 without erasing its failed verification baseline or claiming universal recovery.

## Implemented corrections

| Problem | Change | Verified result |
|---|---|---|
| Four landing guides pass through solid deck material | Four Ø40 mm deck passages around unchanged Ø36 mm guide envelopes | Nominal 2 mm radial clearance |
| Four landing guides enter electronics allowances | Raise both intact electronics volumes by 10 mm | 8 mm guide/equipment separation; 27 mm roof allowance |
| Two rails overlap the deck by 1.5 mm | Seat rails on the actual upper deck surface | Two volumetric overlaps removed |
| Load model would omit equipment relocation | Raise the modeled COM by a conservative full 10 mm | 1,080 nominal cases pass at ≤5° under the declared assumptions |
| Support-loss feasibility is only reported after analysis | Add a phase preflight that checks every sampled extra-foot-loss case | All six current phases fail the static contingency gate |
| Fault handling might attempt an infeasible hold or blind foot placement | Add an offline monitor with latched faults and explicit six-contact rearm | Lost/stale/invalid feedback cannot automatically resume the sequence |

All 86 components remain. Commercial envelope volumes are unchanged. Only the four specified deck passages remove modeled material; the approximately 40.7 g at assumed aluminum density receives no payload or mass-budget credit. The 60 kg limit and 56.335 kg allocation remain unchanged. Raising the entire modeled COM by 10 mm conservatively bounds the equipment move only under the original mass assumptions; actual COM and mass still require measurement.

The new [STEP packaging model](../../cad/cots_v1_1/rover_packaging.step) and [exchange checks](../../cad/cots_v1_1/cad_checks.json) are generated by `cad/scripts/build_cots_revision.py`. It imports the unchanged original generator, applies only the declared chassis changes, and leaves the unresolved joints visible. The STEP roundtrip retains 86 valid solids. It is not a fabrication drawing.

## Interfaces that cannot yet be closed

The [per-pair issue ledger](../../verification/cots/tranche_03/results.json) accounts for all original 70 findings. The remaining 60 are ten interfaces per leg:

| Family | Pairs | Required resolution |
|---|---:|---|
| Motor/carrier | 18 | Detailed supported shaft, bearing shoulders/retention and torque coupling; verify alignment and motor load isolation |
| Carrier/link stock | 30 | Actual carrier walls, clevises, shaft hubs and retained joints; demonstrate the complete structural load path |
| Femur/tibia stock at knee | 6 | Articulating clevis geometry and swept clearance through the declared joint range |
| Tibia/ATI sensor | 6 | Adapter and gimbal geometry using the ordered sensor drawing; no structural bypass around the transducer |

Moving a motor or trimming a tube to remove an overlap would not establish those load paths. These envelopes stay until complete replacement assemblies, joint loads, tolerances and fasteners can be checked together. Manufacturer interface facts and selected components remain in [the original interface control document](../cots_v1/interfaces.md) and [source register](../cots_v1/sources.json).

The deck passages provide clearance, not guide support. Guide mounting flanges, deck reinforcement, fasteners, side-load restraint, damping peak reactions and mechanical park stops still need design and proof. The 2 mm passage clearance is nominal; no tolerance stack has been verified. Moving the rails also requires revised chassis/hip attachment drawings. Cable bend radii, connector access and fastener geometry remain absent. No structural strength result is claimed for the perforated deck.

## Corrected fault-handling contract

`models/cots_support_guard.py` has no motor, CAN, contactor or E-stop I/O. It provides two separate offline checks:

1. **Phase preflight:** evaluate the full sampled phase, every declared COM/gravity case, and every additional supporting-foot removal. Solver success alone is insufficient: support margin, COP, torque, normal load and equilibrium residuals must also pass. Missing samples or invalid phases fail closed. A static contingency pass would still require dynamic and physical evidence before operation. The current six phases all fail it.
2. **Latched monitor:** start disarmed; explicitly arm with six valid contacts under the external restraint. Declare a swing before lifting its foot. Losing any expected support, invalid feedback, stale motor/contact feedback at 40 ms, or host heartbeat age at 100 ms latches a fault and inhibits advance. Neither a recovered packet nor relabeling the lost foot as the new swing clears it. Explicit rearm requires six fresh contacts and restraint confirmation. An explicit six-contact transfer separates swing legs.

The contact flags must eventually come from an independently calibrated contact estimator. No force threshold, debounce time or sensor uncertainty has been invented. The new 40 ms contact freshness threshold is a design requirement for integration, not measured Ethernet timing. The monitor can only inspect delivered snapshots; a real independent watchdog must detect complete host silence. Simulation restraint confirmation does not validate a physical catch.

**There is no automatic command to hold an impossible posture, drop the swing foot, disable torque or open a contactor.** Each could worsen a real fall or regenerative event. A tested restrained response and qualified independent catch must exist before hardware operation. Both utilities always report `hardware_motion_allowed: false`; they cannot certify hardware through a caller flag.
